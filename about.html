The Public Health Professional
Bridging Knowledge and Action

Mercy Ogunwale is a public health researcher, community health practitioner, and founder based in Ibadan, Nigeria. She holds a First Class BSc in Public Health from Lead City University, Ibadan (2024) and works across three things she genuinely cares about: field research, digital health, and helping people grow.

Her fieldwork has taken her across communities in Oyo State, where she has worked on HPV awareness campaigns, run health education sessions for school girls, and collected data directly from HIV patients. These were not desk jobs. They required her to show up, earn trust, and have real conversations with people in situations that matter. That experience shaped how she thinks about research — not as something done to communities, but with them.

She has administered interviews and surveys across a wide range of public health studies, engaging hundreds of respondents across different populations. On the technical side, she works with SPSS, R, Python, Power BI, KoboToolbox, and Google Forms and has used all of them in actual research, not just in theory.

She is the founder of EPIX Initiative, a research and development organisation focused on training the next generation of African health researchers and building digital health tools designed for the communities that need them most. She also founded CeeWriting Service, where she has trained over 100 people on CV writing and personal branding, and has worked one-on-one with students who needed help figuring out what they actually want to do with their careers.

During her NYSC service year, she worked as a Community Health Lead, taking her public health work into grassroots settings and getting things done without the luxury of ideal conditions.

She is the kind of researcher who also builds things, trains people, and shows up in the field. That combination is what makes her work stick.

"My real job in life is to inspire and empower somebody else."
