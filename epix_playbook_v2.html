Welcome
Hey.
This one
is for you.

Nobody gave you a manual when puberty started. Nobody sat you down and explained what was going to happen to your body, your mind, and your emotions. Most of the adults around you probably hoped you would just figure it out on your own.

The truth is, most boys do try to figure it out on their own. They search the internet, ask their friends, or keep everything buried inside. None of those things really work, and a lot of them lead to confusion, shame, and silence.

This playbook exists because you deserve better than that. It covers the things that actually affect you: what is happening to your body, sexual health, your mental wellbeing, how technology affects you, and how to navigate relationships without losing yourself.

"Growing up is not about pretending you have it all figured out. It is about being honest enough to admit when you do not."

Chapter 01 · Your Body Is Changing
05
Adolescent Health
What is puberty, and why is it happening to me?

Puberty is the process your body goes through to become an adult. It is driven by hormones, specifically testosterone, which your body starts producing in much larger amounts from around age 9 to 14. Testosterone is responsible for almost every physical change you are noticing.

It does not happen at the same time or speed for everyone. Some boys start early, some start later. Neither is wrong. Comparing yourself to friends or classmates will only frustrate you, because each body has its own timeline.

What changes to expect
  • 1

    Height and muscle growth. You may grow several centimetres in a single year. Your muscles also develop, especially if you are physically active.

  • 2

    Voice changes. Your voice will deepen and may crack unpredictably for a while. This is temporary and completely normal.

  • 3

    Body hair. Hair will appear under your arms, on your face, chest, legs, and around your genitals. The amount and pattern is genetic.

  • 4

    Genital development. Your penis and testicles will grow larger. This is normal and usually one of the first changes to happen.

  • 5

    Skin changes. Increased oil production means acne is common. Washing your face twice a day and not squeezing pimples helps manage it.

  • 6

    Sweating more. Your sweat glands become more active. Daily bathing and deodorant are not optional during puberty.

9–14
The age range when most boys start puberty. Starting a little earlier or later is still within the normal range.
Did you know?

Testosterone does not just affect your body. It also affects your mood, energy levels, and how competitive or risk-taking you feel. Understanding this helps you manage those feelings instead of being controlled by them.

Wet dreams: what nobody told you

A wet dream, or nocturnal emission, is when you ejaculate during sleep. It is not something you can control, and it is not something to be ashamed of. It is simply your body testing out its new reproductive functions. It happens to almost every male at some point during puberty.

About erections

Random, unexpected erections are very common during puberty. They can be triggered by almost anything, or nothing at all. They are not a sign of anything being wrong. They reduce in frequency as you get older.

Hygiene: the basics that actually matter

Nobody is born knowing how to look after an adult body. Here is a practical guide for taking care of yourself during puberty and beyond.

01
Daily bathing
Shower every day, especially after physical activity. Pay extra attention to armpits, groin, and feet. Use mild soap.
02
Deodorant
Deodorant masks smell. Antiperspirant reduces sweating. Either works, but you must use it consistently every day.
03
Face care
Wash morning and night with a gentle face wash. Do not pop pimples. If acne is severe, see a doctor. It is treatable.
PRACTICAL HYGIENE · Project SHIELD students practicing correct hand sanitation methods using soap.
Genital hygiene

This is rarely talked about but very important. The area around and under the foreskin (if you are uncircumcised) can collect a white substance called smegma if not cleaned regularly. Pull back the foreskin gently and wash with water during your bath. No harsh soap is needed inside that area.

Keep the groin area clean and dry to prevent fungal infections, which are common in hot and humid climates like Nigeria. Change your underwear daily and choose cotton over synthetic materials.

Myth

Boys do not need to wash under the foreskin. It cleans itself.

Fact

The foreskin area requires gentle manual cleaning to prevent infection and odour. Water alone is enough.

Oral health

Brush your teeth twice a day, morning and before bed, for at least two minutes each time. Use fluoride toothpaste. If you have access to floss or mouthwash, use them. Bad breath during adolescence is common but manageable.

When to see a doctor

Pain, swelling, or unusual lumps in the testicles (do a self-check once a month)

Discharge or unusual smell from the penis not related to hygiene

Severe acne that over-the-counter products cannot manage

No signs of puberty by age 14, or puberty that seems to stop partway through

Breast tissue development (usually temporary but a doctor should confirm)

Chapter 02 · Sexual Health, Straight Talk
08
Consent: the one thing that is never negotiable

Consent means that everyone involved in a sexual activity genuinely and freely agrees to it. It is not about the absence of a no. It is about the clear presence of a yes.

Consent must be
FreeNot given under pressure, manipulation, or while under the influence of alcohol or drugs
ReversibleAnyone can change their mind at any point, even if they said yes before
InformedBoth people understand what they are agreeing to
EnthusiasticNot just tolerance, but genuine willingness
SpecificAgreeing to one thing does not mean agreeing to everything

Silence is not consent. Not saying no is not consent. Being in a relationship is not automatic consent. If you are ever unsure, ask.

Sexually transmitted infections (STIs)

STIs are infections passed from one person to another through sexual contact. They include HIV, gonorrhoea, syphilis, chlamydia, herpes, and others. Many STIs have no visible symptoms, which means someone can have one without knowing it. The only way to know for certain is to get tested.

Myth

You can tell if someone has an STI by looking at them.

Fact

Most STIs have no visible signs. The only reliable way to know is testing at a clinic.

Condoms: how and why

A condom is the only contraceptive method that protects against both unwanted pregnancy and STIs at the same time. When used correctly and consistently, it is highly effective.

  • 1

    Check the expiry date on the wrapper before use. An expired condom is more likely to break.

  • 2

    Open the wrapper carefully. Do not use your teeth or scissors, as these can damage the condom.

  • 3

    Pinch the tip of the condom before rolling it down to leave space for semen. Rolling without pinching increases the risk of breaking.

  • 4

    Roll it all the way to the base of the erect penis. If it does not roll down easily, it is on inside out. Use a new one.

  • 5

    After sex, hold the base while withdrawing to prevent spillage. Dispose of it properly. Do not flush it.

About pornography

Pornography does not represent real sex. The bodies, the duration, and the expectations shown are produced for an audience. Using it as a reference sets you up for unrealistic expectations and can harm how you see women and relationships.

Remember
There is no timeline by which you have to be sexually active. Anyone who pressures you into sex is not someone you should be intimate with.

Mental Health
Why do I feel things so intensely right now?

During adolescence, the prefrontal cortex, which is the part of the brain responsible for decision-making, emotional regulation, and impulse control, is still developing. It does not fully mature until your mid-twenties. This means that the emotional intensity you feel right now is partly neurological. You are not too sensitive. You are not overreacting. Your brain is literally not yet fully equipped to process emotions the way an adult's brain does.

On top of that, testosterone increases emotional reactivity. Combined with social pressure, academic stress, family expectations, and comparison to peers, it is no wonder that adolescence can feel overwhelming at times.

Anger: what it is really about

Boys are often socialised to express emotions through anger because it is seen as acceptable and even "masculine." But a lot of the time, anger is a secondary emotion. Underneath anger is usually hurt, fear, embarrassment, or helplessness. Learning to identify what is underneath your anger is one of the most useful things you can do for yourself.

"The reason boys seem angry more than sad is because they have been taught that anger is the only acceptable face of pain."

Depression: more than just sadness

Depression is not just feeling sad for a day or two. It is a persistent state where sadness, emptiness, or numbness lasts for weeks, interferes with daily life, and does not improve with time. Common signs in adolescent males include:

Persistent low mood or irritability lasting more than two weeks

Loss of interest in things you previously enjoyed

Changes in sleep: either too much or not enough

Feeling worthless, hopeless, or like a burden to others

Difficulty concentrating in school or making decisions

Withdrawing from people and activities

If you recognise several of these signs lasting more than two weeks, please speak to a trusted adult or a health professional. Depression is medical and it is treatable.

55%
of adolescents living with HIV in one Nigerian study showed signs of depression, yet most had never spoken about it to anyone
Anxiety

Anxiety is your brain's alarm system going off too frequently or too intensely. It becomes a problem when the anxiety is constant, disproportionate to the actual situation, or stops you from doing things you need to do.

Signs of anxiety

Racing thoughts you cannot switch off, tight chest, feeling like something bad is about to happen for no reason, avoiding situations out of fear, and physical symptoms like nausea, headaches, or muscle tension.

Healthy coping habits
  • 1

    Physical exercise. Even 30 minutes of walking reduces stress hormones significantly.

  • 2

    Sleep. Adolescents need 8 to 10 hours. Sleep deprivation amplifies every negative emotion.

  • 3

    Talking. Start with: "I have not been feeling great lately."

  • 4

    Limiting alcohol and substances. They worsen mood and anxiety significantly.

The questions boys ask
but nobody answers

These are real questions. They deserve real answers.

Is it normal for a guy to cry?
Yes. Crying is a physiological stress response that exists in every human being regardless of gender. The idea that boys should not cry was invented socially, not biologically. Suppressing tears does not make you stronger. It just keeps emotional pressure building inside you with nowhere to go.
Sometimes I feel like nobody would notice if I disappeared. Is that normal?
Feeling invisible or disconnected is something many adolescents experience. But if this thought comes with a feeling that you do not want to be here, please tell someone you trust immediately. Those feelings are signals that you need support, not signals to stay silent. You are not a burden for needing help.
My family says I should just pray and stop being weak. How do I explain that it is not that simple?
Mental health struggles are not a sign of weak faith or weak character. They are medical experiences, just like malaria or a broken bone. Faith can be a powerful source of comfort, but it does not replace the need for proper support. You can honour your faith and still seek help. They are not opposites.
I feel angry all the time and I do not know why.
Persistent anger without a clear cause is often a sign of something deeper: accumulated stress, unexpressed pain, or depression presenting as irritability. Tracking when it happens and what preceded it can help you start to understand it.
How do I talk to someone about how I am feeling without it being awkward?
Start small. You do not need to deliver a full emotional breakdown the first time. Try something like: "I have been feeling stressed lately" or "I have been going through something difficult." Most people will naturally follow up. The hardest part is almost always the first sentence.
I use games, music, or my phone to escape when I feel bad. Is that okay?
Using distractions to get temporary relief from difficult emotions is normal. The problem comes when it becomes the only way you cope, and you never actually deal with the underlying feeling. Distractions are fine occasionally. They are not a long-term solution.
Can boys get eating disorders or body image problems?
Absolutely. Boys are increasingly affected by body image pressures, especially around muscularity and fitness. Obsessive gym habits, restricting food, or excessive supplement use can all be signs of disordered thinking around the body.
The bottom line

Seeking help for your mental health is not weakness. It is one of the most self-aware and courageous things a person can do. The boys who learn this early live better, fuller, and more honest lives.

Chapter 04 · Screens, Phones & You

Social media and your self-image

Social media shows you the highlight reel of everyone else's life. What you see is what people chose to post: the best photos, the fun moments, the achievements. You do not see the failed attempts, the anxiety, the loneliness, or the filters and editing that went into most of those images.

When you compare your internal experience to someone else's curated external presentation, you will almost always feel worse about yourself. This is not a character flaw. It is the natural result of an unequal comparison.

What you see

Everyone else seems confident, popular, and living their best life.

The reality

Most people online are performing a version of themselves they wish was true, not the full picture.

Screen time and sleep

Blue light from screens suppresses melatonin, the hormone that makes you feel sleepy. Using your phone within an hour of bedtime delays sleep onset, reduces deep sleep quality, and means you wake up less rested even after 8 hours. Over time, poor sleep significantly worsens mood, concentration, and emotional regulation.

  • 1

    Put your phone in a different room 45 to 60 minutes before you sleep.

  • 2

    Charge it outside your bedroom. The bedroom should be for sleep, not scrolling.

  • 3

    Turn off notifications for social media apps. Check them on your own schedule.

Online safety

The internet creates situations that can feel private but are not. Here are things every adolescent male should know:

Images and videos
Never send intimate images of yourself to anyone, even someone you trust. Once an image leaves your device, you lose control of it permanently.
Strangers online
People online are not always who they say they are. Be cautious about sharing your location, school, or personal details with people you have not met in person.
Cyberbullying
If someone is harassing you online, screenshot it and tell a trusted adult. Blocking is not weakness. Protecting your peace is intelligence.
Gambling and betting apps
Sports betting platforms are designed to be addictive. Many adolescents develop compulsive betting habits that cause serious financial and psychological harm.
When to tell an adult immediately

If someone online asks you to meet in person, sends sexual content, asks you to keep conversations secret, or makes you uncomfortable in any way, tell a trusted adult right away. These are not normal internet interactions.

Friendships
The friends you choose will shape you more than you realise

Research consistently shows that the people you spend the most time with have a significant influence on your habits, your beliefs, and your future choices. This is not about judging others. It is about being intentional about who you allow close to you.

Signs of a healthy friendship

You can be yourself without performing. They support you without requiring you to compromise your values. Disagreements are possible without the friendship ending. You feel better about yourself after spending time with them, not worse.

Peer pressure: how it actually works

Most peer pressure is not someone physically forcing you to do something. It is more subtle: the feeling that if you do not go along with what the group is doing, you will be left out, mocked, or seen as weak. That feeling is real, but it is not a reliable guide for decision-making.

"The boys who follow the crowd because they are afraid of being alone end up lonelier than the ones who stood apart."

Family dynamics

Family can be a source of enormous support, but it can also be a source of real stress. Arguments with parents, absent fathers, pressure to provide, academic expectations, and feeling misunderstood at home are all experiences that many adolescent boys in Nigeria navigate.

You cannot control what your family does. You can control how you respond, and over time, you can choose the kind of family environment you want to build for yourself as an adult.

Romantic Relationships
What healthy attraction and relationships look like

Attraction is normal. Wanting romantic connection is normal. What matters is how you pursue it and what you do when it does not go the way you hoped.

  • 1

    Respect before anything else. Interest in someone does not give you entitlement to their time, attention, or body. A relationship begins with mutual respect, not pursuit.

  • 2

    Rejection is not an attack. If someone is not interested, that is their right. How you handle rejection says a great deal about your character. Walk away with dignity.

  • 3

    Communicate honestly. Most relationship problems come from things left unsaid. If something bothers you, say it directly and calmly rather than pulling away or acting out.

  • 4

    There is no rush. You do not need to be in a relationship to prove your worth. Emotional maturity, not relationship status, is what actually impresses people in the long run.

Toxic belief

A real man does not catch feelings or show vulnerability in relationships.

The truth

The ability to be emotionally present is what builds lasting, meaningful relationships.

On masculinity
Being a man is not about being the loudest, the toughest, or the most sexually active. It is about being someone others can rely on, including yourself. Character is built in private moments when nobody is watching.
